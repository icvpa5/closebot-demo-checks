## "ai sdr demo" is really three different searches

The keyword covers a few intentions that get mashed together:

- **Book a demo.** You know roughly what AI SDRs do, you want someone to show you their version on a call, ideally with your own use case in mind.
- **Watch a demo.** You want a recorded walkthrough to see whether the thing is a real product or a landing page with stock photos.
- **Run one yourself.** You want to build or test an AI setter on your own leads, on your own CRM, and judge it from actual conversations rather than a curated screen share.

The third option is the one that's changed most in the last couple of years. Tools like CloseBot will hand you a free account and an auto-built starter agent, so the "demo" is a test conversation with your own fictional lead instead of a scheduled Zoom.

## What an AI SDR demo actually shows, and what it can't

"AI SDR" gets used for two products that barely resemble each other.

On one end you have outbound email machines. AiSDR, for example, positions itself around finding in-market buyers from signals and sending researched outreach, with the pitch that you get 1–3 demos per 100 leads. That's a sequencing and copywriting problem wearing an AI hat.

On the other end you have inbound conversation tools that sit inside your CRM's text channels, qualify whoever shows up, and put a meeting on a calendar. Fin for Sales is a pure inbound play. CloseBot sits in the same category but with a different construction: it's a no-code agent builder for agencies and businesses, mostly used on top of GoHighLevel and HubSpot, that works across SMS, website chat, email and other text channels inside the CRM you already pay for.

A demo from either camp will look impressive in the same way. The difference shows up in three places: how the agent behaves when the lead goes off-script, who maintains the flows after launch, and what you pay per message once real volume arrives.

Also worth knowing before you book anything: voice is a separate product category. CloseBot is text-only. If your demo requirement is "AI answers the phone and books", that's GoHighLevel Voice AI or a voice-first vendor, not a chat agent.

## Seven questions that separate a working demo from a slideshow

Ask these on the call. The answers tell you more than the demo screen does.

1. **Does the agent work from objectives or from a script?** Objective-driven agents receive a goal ("qualify this lead, collect these fields, book this calendar slot") and adapt the wording. Scripted flows break the moment a prospect types something unexpected.
2. **What happens when the AI can't answer?** The honest answer is usually some form of escalation. CloseBot handles this with Smart FAQ, which flags unanswered questions and then re-engages every lead who asked once you've supplied the answer. Ask any vendor what their version of that loop is.
3. **Can a human take over mid-conversation?** You want a pause button, not a support ticket.
4. **Which channels does it actually cover?** "Omni-channel" often means SMS plus web chat. Ask specifically about email, WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, and whatever your CRM exposes.
5. **What does a month of real usage cost?** Get the per-message rate, the included volume, the overage rate, and the seat and storage charges. These usually matter more than the base subscription.
6. **Who owns the configuration?** If every change requires the vendor, you're renting a black box. If it's a drag-and-drop builder you control, you're buying a tool.
7. **What's the setup time for a genuinely custom flow?** "Minutes" is marketing. "Same day for a simple qualifier, a week for a multi-branch flow with calendar routing" is information.

## Where most demos quietly stop

The demo ends at the appointment confirmation screen. The parts that decide whether the AI SDR pays for itself are less photogenic.

**Cost per message.** CloseBot's Business plans include message costs in the base price, with a 500-message monthly ceiling on any paid Business plan and the option to raise that ceiling for a higher monthly fee, taking advantage of bulk pricing as you go. Go past your ceiling and you pay overage at a 2x rate drawn from a wallet. On the Free plan you're capped at 100 messages, then $0.08 per message. On Agency plans it's a flat $0.012 per message, rebillable.

**Seats and storage.** Extra users cost $5 per seat per month on both Business and Agency plans. Storage is included at 1 MB and you add more as an add-on; CloseBot's docs price Business storage add-ons from $0.10 to $3.00 per MB per month depending on volume, while Agency plans are billed at $0.006 per MB per day with the option to mark it up.

**Token costs.** This is the quiet line item. CloseBot charges for its own message handling, and the LLM costs from providers like OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Grok or DeepSeek sit on top of that depending on configuration. CloseBot's own pricing breakdown gives a scaled example: 102 sub-accounts, 24,720 messages in a month, 50 MB of uploaded knowledge, and OpenAI as the provider came out to roughly $809/month all in — with the note that switching to DeepSeek on that same workload would have landed closer to $617.

That gap between $397 of subscription and $809 of reality is the single most useful thing a vendor can explain to you in a demo. Most don't volunteer it.

## Business plan or Agency plan?

The split is straightforward, and it's the part most buyers get wrong by overpaying.

**Take a Business plan** if the agent is qualifying your own inbound leads. Same agents, same channels, same integrations, just aimed at your pipeline. A single-location home services company getting 50 leads a month is comfortably inside the message ceiling, and message costs are already baked into the subscription.

**Take an Agency plan** if you'll resell the setup. The $397/month buys you the re-billing machinery rather than more AI: connect your Stripe account, enable re-billing, set your own markup on messages, seats and storage, and clients top up a wallet that pays you. CloseBot's own docs show the wholesale rates you're marking up — $0.012 per message, $5.00 per seat per month, $0.006 per MB per day of storage — and the pages describing the agency model claim agencies billing as little as $100/month per client up to $10k+ monthly.

The Business plan won't show you re-billing or white-labeling at all, so don't start there if your plan is to sell AI setters as a service and migrate later.

## When to skip the demo entirely

Book a vendor demo when you're comparing three tools at enterprise scale, when procurement needs a security review, or when your use case has compliance constraints you need in writing. That's what Growth-tier conversations with SLAs and quarterly audits are for.

Skip it when what you actually need is to see whether an AI setter can handle your leads. In that case the fastest honest answer comes from opening a free account, connecting a CRM source, pointing the starter agent at a real calendar, and having a colleague message it as a fake lead. You'll learn more in twenty minutes of that than in an hour of someone else's screen share, and you'll find out before you've paid anything.
